VERIFIED — todo_030dca12cfea (COALESCE-GROWTH), and this ticket overturned my own published claim.

## What it found

`real-network.md` §3.3a recorded `measure_real_journal` FAILING at the production 100 ms window with
`coalescing must not grow the lane`, and read that as "the envelope makes the published lane larger".
I carried that reading into the PR body and the final report as "a real defect".

Measured properly, THERE IS NO PRODUCT DEFECT. The lane was byte-identical:
`{"events":19,"todayBytes":9196,"coalescedBytes":9196,"published":19,"published":19,"ratio":1.0}`.

## Mechanism, verified by me in the source

- `coalesce.rs:208` — `if pending.merged == 0 { out.push(pending.event); return; }`: a lone fragment is
  handed back unchanged, so a merged envelope CANNOT land on a single event. I read this line myself.
- `COALESCE_WINDOW = 100 ms` (`coalesce.rs:59`);
- that journal's `text_chunk` gaps are **100.9-110 ms**, i.e. every fragment arrives after its
  predecessor's window closed. Nothing *may* merge.
- the old assertion was strict (`coalesced_bytes < today_bytes`), which no journal that cannot merge
  can ever satisfy. THAT was the defect — the measurement, not the coalescer.

## Why the first reading was a guess

The driver printed its marker AFTER its assertions, so the failing run reported no numbers at all. The
ticket made printing-before-asserting permanent and then measured both cases:

| journal (real stack) | gaps | window | publishes | bytes | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slow (stub 100 ms/token) | 100.9-110 ms | 100 ms (prod) | 19/19 | 9196 -> 9196 | nothing to merge; unchanged is correct |
| same journal | same | 5000 ms | 7/19 | 9196 -> 4748 (1.94x) | merge path correct |
| burst (stub 20 ms/token) | 8-30 ms | **100 ms (prod)** | 21/69 | 31375 -> 13666 (2.30x) | the window does its job |

The burst journal is the case the original finding was really about, and it shows the production window
merging 2.30x with every invariant held (69 parties, no character lost, newest index published).

## The transferable lesson

A failing assertion is a claim about the CODE until the instrument is shown to report what it measured.
This run's numbers were printed after the assertions, so the failure carried no evidence - and a guess
became a "real defect" that reached two published documents. Recorded for the FLAKES/PLAN rules: a
measurement driver must print its numbers BEFORE it asserts anything about them.
